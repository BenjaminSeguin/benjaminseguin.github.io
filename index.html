About me

I like the part of research that ends in working code: reading a paper until the result actually makes sense, implementing it, and finding out where it holds up on real data and where it does not. That loop is what I want to keep doing, and it matters more to me than the particular subject it is applied to.

I recently completed an M.Sc. in Applied Maths and Computer Science (IASD) at Université PSL. My research internship, in the MILES team at LAMSADE with Clément W. Royer, was on nonconvex optimization for data science: I proposed a first-order method, proved its complexity guarantees, and validated it numerically. Alongside it I explored nonconvex inverse problems in imaging, and their extensions to optimal transport.

Before turning to machine learning I spent four years in quantitative finance in Montréal: an M.Sc. in Financial Engineering at HEC, three open-source R packages written with David Ardia and published on CRAN, and a year of quantitative research at La Caisse, where I built machine learning pipelines for LLM-based sentiment extraction.

I am now looking for a CIFRE PhD or a research-oriented position. What draws me is working close to researchers on questions that are still open — optimization is where I learned to do research, not a subject I am tied to, and I am glad to pick up a new field.
